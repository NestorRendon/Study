If it's not rendering properly on your end, here it is as a plain markdown table:  

| Term | Category | Definition | Tools | Why managers care | Critical view |
| ---------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Status Quo | Culture | The current state of things — how work is being done right now. Often referenced when someone wants to challenge or preserve existing practices. | Retros, OKR planning, roadmap discussions | Leaders use it to frame change management: are we challenging it or protecting it? | Often weaponised to resist change or overstate urgency. Ask what evidence supports changing it. |
| PR (Pull Request) | Process | A developer's formal request to merge their code branch into the main codebase. Triggers review, discussion, and automated checks. | GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket — linked from Jira | Managers track PR throughput and age to spot bottlenecks. | Large, long-lived PRs become review nightmares. If PRs sit open for days, it signals poor async culture or overloaded reviewers. |
| Peer Review | Quality | Having another engineer review your work before it's merged or shipped. Catches bugs, shares knowledge, enforces standards. | GitHub PR reviews, Confluence, Figma comments | Reduces defect rate, spreads ownership, prevents knowledge silos. | Can become a rubber-stamp if culture doesn't reward thorough feedback. Reviewers under pressure approve things they haven't truly read. |
| Unit Test | Quality | An automated test that checks a single, isolated piece of code works as expected. Part of the broader testing pyramid. | Jest, JUnit, PyTest — results in CI/CD pipelines | High coverage gives confidence to refactor and ship faster. Low coverage signals technical debt. | Coverage % is a vanity metric — 90% coverage can still mean a broken product if tests don't test meaningful behaviour. |
| Cycle Time | Metrics | Elapsed time from when a developer starts a ticket to when it's deployed to production. One of the four DORA metrics. | Jira cycle time reports, LinearB, Jellyfish, Swarmia | Lower cycle time = faster feedback loops. Managers track it to find where work gets stuck. | Optimising cycle time alone can encourage rushed work and skipped reviews. Read it alongside change failure rate. |
| Evangelise | Culture | To actively promote a technology, practice, or idea internally or externally. Common for platform teams and architecture leads. | Confluence, Slack, tech talks, blog posts | Adoption of new tools rarely happens without internal champions. Leaders look for people who can persuade without authority. | Evangelism without listening becomes preaching. The best advocates shape the thing they're promoting, not just sell it. |
| Metrics for Releases | Metrics | Quantitative measures for release health — deployment frequency, change failure rate, MTTR. | Jira, Datadog, PagerDuty, Grafana | Replaces gut-feel with trend lines for resourcing and process decisions. | Teams can game metrics (e.g. splitting tickets to boost deploy frequency). Metrics always need context. |
| E-commerce | Product | Buying and selling online. In tech interviews, means the domain your product lives in: checkout, catalogues, payments, inventory. | Shopify, Stripe, Salesforce Commerce | Signals business model and stakes — e-commerce bugs directly cost revenue. | Used very loosely. Always clarify scope — it can mean a 3-person startup or a 50-team platform org. |
| ALC | Process | Architecture and Leadership Council — a governance body that reviews significant technical decisions before they're committed to. | Confluence (ADRs/RFCs), formal review meetings | Prevents costly rework by catching bad architectural decisions early. | Can become a bureaucratic gatekeeper that slows teams without adding value. The best ones give fast, async decisions. |
| Jira | Tool | Atlassian's project tracking tool. Used to create, assign, prioritise, and track tasks through a workflow. | GitHub, Confluence, Slack integrations | Provides visibility across teams. Managers track sprint progress, blocked tickets, and velocity. | Jira-heavy cultures optimise for ticket-closing over outcomes. Backlog grooming can consume time needed for actual work. |
| Confluence | Tool | Atlassian's wiki and documentation platform. Used for specs, post-mortems, architecture decisions, onboarding guides. | Links from Jira, used alongside Notion, Google Docs | Institutional knowledge lives here. Leaders check if teams document decisions or keep things in people's heads. | Often becomes a graveyard of outdated pages. Stale docs create false confidence — people make decisions based on wrong information. |
| OKRs | Product | Objectives and Key Results — a goal-setting framework. Objective = qualitative ambition; Key Results = measurable outcomes. | Confluence, Notion, Lattice, Weekdone | Aligns teams to company strategy. Every roadmap decision should trace back to an OKR. | Can become a checkbox exercise. If set top-down without team input or never reviewed, they're shelfware with branding. |
| DORA Metrics | Metrics | Four research-backed metrics: Deployment Frequency, Lead Time for Changes, Change Failure Rate, and MTTR. | LinearB, Jellyfish, Faros, Grafana | Gold standard for engineering health. VPs use them to benchmark teams and justify process investments. | Designed for elite DevOps teams. Applying them rigidly to early-stage startups can misrepresent performance. |
| Sprint / Iteration | Process | A fixed-length work cycle (1–2 weeks) in Agile/Scrum. Team commits to tasks, then reviews what shipped at the end. | Jira Sprints, Linear Cycles, Shortcut | Creates a rhythm for planning and feedback. Managers track velocity to forecast roadmap delivery. | Can create artificial urgency and context-switching. Some teams do better with Kanban (continuous flow). |
| Technical Debt | Process | The implied cost of shortcuts taken in code — quick fixes or outdated designs that need to be addressed later. | Jira, SonarQube, Confluence (debt registers) | Unmanaged debt slows future development. Leaders want to see a plan for it. | The "debt" metaphor implies it's always intentional. Often it accumulates from poor decisions, not strategic trade-offs. |
| Retrospective | Collab | A recurring meeting to reflect on what went well, what didn't, and what to change. Core Agile ceremony. | Miro, EasyRetro, Confluence, Jira (action items) | Continuous improvement signal. Leaders look for retros that produce real changes, not just vent sessions. | Becomes performative in psychologically unsafe teams. People raise safe topics instead of real problems. |
| SLA / SLO / SLI | Metrics | SLA = customer-facing commitment. SLO = internal target (e.g. 99.9% uptime). SLI = the actual measurement. | Datadog, PagerDuty, Prometheus, Grafana | Defines what "good" looks like for reliability. Used to decide when to invest in reliability vs features. | SLAs often set without engineering input, creating politically driven targets teams then sandbag or constantly breach. |
| Roadmap | Product | A prioritised plan of what a team will build and when — usually covering the next quarter to year. | Jira Advanced Roadmaps, Productboard, Notion | Communicates priorities to stakeholders. Engineers should understand and challenge items that don't make technical sense. | Creates a false promise of certainty. Stakeholders treat them as contracts; teams treat them as aspirations. |
| Incident / Post-mortem | Process | An incident is an unexpected failure. A post-mortem is the blameless analysis written after — timeline, root cause, prevention. | PagerDuty, Opsgenie, Confluence, Jira | Shows team maturity. Leaders look for blameless post-mortems and whether action items get closed. | Blameless culture is aspirational in many orgs. Post-mortems can become political documents protecting individuals. |
| Bus Factor | Collab | The number of people who'd need to leave for a project to be in serious trouble. Low bus factor = fragile team. | Confluence, pair programming, GitHub CODEOWNERS | Exposes knowledge concentration risk. Leaders actively work to raise this number across critical systems. | Efforts to raise it are often deprioritised under delivery pressure — until someone leaves and the risk becomes real. |
